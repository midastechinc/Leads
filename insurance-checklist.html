Checklist · Canada 2026

Cyber insurance readiness

What Canadian cyber insurers ask small businesses at renewal. Tick each one you can honestly say yes to.

  1. Multi-factor sign-in on every email accountIncluding shared mailboxes, admin accounts and anyone working remotely. Missing MFA is the most common reason applications are declined.
  2. MFA on remote access and cloud appsVPN, remote desktop, accounting and practice software, and any admin portal.
  3. Managed antivirus (EDR) that someone watchesEndpoint detection and response on every computer and server, monitored 24/7, not just free antivirus.
  4. Backups that can't be changed or deletedKept separate from your everyday logins, ideally stored in Canada.
  5. A restore tested in the last 90 daysInsurers increasingly want proof that a backup actually came back, and how long it took.
  6. A written incident response planWho to call, what to shut off, and who must be notified (clients, insurer, privacy commissioner) if you're hacked.
  7. Security awareness training for all staffAt least once a year, ideally with practice phishing emails.
  8. Supported, patched systemsUpdates applied automatically, and no Windows 10 PCs left (free security updates ended October 14, 2025).
  9. Accounts switched off when people leaveSame day, including email, shared passwords and remote access.
  10. Payment and banking changes confirmed by phoneFake "our banking details changed" emails are one of the costliest scams for small businesses.

Every insurer's form is different, often 40 to 80 questions. Answer exactly what's true: an answer that doesn't match reality can lead to a higher price, a refused claim or a cancelled policy. This checklist is a guide, not insurance or legal advice.
